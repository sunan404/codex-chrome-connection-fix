# General academic review layer

Establish the question, contribution, target population/configuration and paper
type before judging adequacy. Distinguish descriptive, predictive, causal,
theoretical and systems performance claims. Match the evaluation to what is claimed.

Assess experimental construct validity (does the metric operationalize the goal?),
internal validity (controls, confounders, leakage, intervention), external validity
(sampling and transfer boundaries), and reliability (variation across independent
units and repeated conditions). For systems, include workload realism, resource
budgets, throughput/latency tradeoffs, failures and measurement overhead.

Baselines should address the claimed contribution under comparable information,
tuning and resources. Explain why a proposed sanity baseline would adjudicate a
particular claim. Do not demand a named algorithm from memory as a universal rule.
An ablation changes one intended factor; changing several factors limits attribution.

Seek strengths as well as concerns: credible controls, interpretable effect sizes,
transparent constraints, null results under sound designs, reproducible procedures,
useful framing and clear exposition. Describe novelty positioning within this
manuscript unless actual literature retrieval was performed.

For venue mode, record venue and year, official rubric URL, retrieval date, exact
relevant passages and the additional dimensions applied. Without a verified rubric,
use general academic review and disclose that limitation. Venue criteria never
replace the research audit. Qualitative overall assessment is optional and late.
